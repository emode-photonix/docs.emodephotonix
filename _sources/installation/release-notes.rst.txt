
Release Notes
^^^^^^^^^^^^^

**1.0.6**

* Changed

  * On Apple silicon, the sparse direct solver is rslab 1.1.2 (from 1.1.0).
  * The Intel Mac installer continues, although the 1.0.5 notes announced 1.0.5 as its last release.
  * Improved EME junctions between mode-solved cross-sections: they conserve power and converge as modes are added, especially at large mode numbers, and across the mode solvers and their meshes. Results can move slightly from 1.0.5.
  * A taper or GDS section now starts refinement from slices placed where its cross-section changes (``EME_settings(initial_slices='auto')``, the new default), read from its rasterized refractive index: none along a straight run, evenly along a linear taper, densely inside a short fast feature, and at every corner of a GDS outline. Refinement still runs afterward and decides the final slicing, so results change only within its tolerance, and it takes fewer rounds. ``EME_settings(initial_slices='single')`` restores the one-slice start of earlier versions.
  * ``EME()`` and ``plot()`` use less memory.

* New

  * ``plot(normalization='section')`` scales each section of a 'z-x' or 'z-y' panel (a straight, a taper, a free-space gap) by its own peak, so a section that carries little of the launched field, such as the free-space gap after an angled facet, is still visible. The 'Norm' selector in the plot window offers it too. Brightness is then not comparable across sections.
  * ``plot(view='layout')``, the new default, draws the 'z-x' panel as the chip layout: every section where its connections place it, a bend as an arc and the sections after it turned with it, the field following the guide, both axes in um. ``view='simulation'`` is the unrolled display of earlier versions, and the 'Physical space' checkbox switches between them.
  * ``check_layout()`` reports overlapping sections, a section whose outline crosses itself, how far each loop of connected sections misses closing, and sections no connection reaches. ``EME()`` runs it first and refuses an overlap or a loop that misses by more than ``minimum_z_step`` before solving anything, and warns about a self-intersecting outline.
  * ``sweep(key='wavelength', values=..., result='S_matrix', method='interpolated')`` sweeps a circuit's S-matrix over hundreds of wavelengths for the cost of a few: the circuit is refined once, each cross-section is solved at ``num_nodes`` wavelengths (default 8), and the S-matrix at every requested wavelength is rebuilt from propagation constants and junctions interpolated between them. ``sweep_data`` reports the node count and the interpolation residual at a held-out node, with a warning above ``interpolation_tolerance``.
  * ``bend_section(name, profile, radius, sweep_angle, method)`` declares a circular bend (``sweep_angle`` in radians, positive toward +x), solved with the bend in cylindrical coordinates (``method='cylindrical'``, the default) or by transformation optics (``method='TO'``). Sections declared after it continue from its end. An anisotropic crystal turns with the guide: a bend in x-cut lithium niobate is sliced along its heading, and a section placed after a turn sees the crystal turned. A bend of an isotropic material is one mode solve. ``copy_section`` raises when a copy's placement would show the crystal differently from its source, rather than reusing the wrong S-matrix.
  * Monitors: ``monitor(name, w, y, angle, length, resolution)`` declares a plane inside the chain (output only, not a port), ``monitor_field(name)`` returns the solved field on it for 1 W launched with the power through it, and ``monitor_coupling(name, mode_field_diameter, angle)`` its coupling to a tilted Gaussian beam such as a fiber mode, the figure a grating coupler is designed for. By reciprocity the same number is the coupling from the fiber into the waveguide. ``sweep(result='monitor_coupling', monitor=, beam=)`` sweeps the beam's angle, size or position without solving again, or any other key with a solve at each value, and ``plot()`` draws each monitor's plane on the 'z-y' panel. The website has a new example, 'EME: Grating Coupler'.
  * A new website example, 'Bend Mode Solvers': the SiN bend of 'Bending: SiN' solved by the FDM with ``bend_method='cylindrical'`` and ``'TO'`` on four grids and by the FEM on six fixed meshes, with each solve's effective index, its error against the finest FEM mesh, its bend loss and its runtime, and plots of the effective-index and bend-loss errors against runtime.
  * The 'Bending: SiN' example uses the window and PMLs of 'Bend Mode Solvers': PMLs on the bend's outer, top and bottom walls (1 µm, ``pml_kappa_max=1``), 2 µm of cladding and 1.5 µm of trench, and a ``max_effective_index`` just above the guided mode.
  * A new website example, 'EME: TFLN Ring Resonator': a racetrack ring in x-cut thin-film lithium niobate, closed with ``connect_ports``, its coupler's bus and ring arms windowed ports, the through-port spectrum over several free spectral ranges from ``sweep(method='interpolated')``, with the free spectral range, extinction and loaded quality factor of each resonance, and the field built up around the ring on resonance, drawn on the chip.
  * Pulsed nonlinear propagation through an EME chain (EMode3D). ``settings(excitation=PulsedSource(...))`` declares a femtosecond pulse, ``straight_section(nonlinear=PulsedProcess(...))`` or ``taper_section(...)`` a section's nonlinearity and poling (``Poling``: a period, a period varying along the section, or explicit domain walls), and ``EME()`` refines the chain, solves each slice at a set of sample wavelengths and propagates the pulse through every slice and junction. ``get('pulse_response')`` returns the spectrum of every tracked mode along the chain, with ``band_energy()`` and ``band_power()`` for the energy and average power in a wavelength band. ``pulse_propagate(poling=...)`` reruns the propagation with another poling without solving any mode again. ``gds_section(nonlinear=PulsedProcess(...))`` declares a pulse's nonlinearity on a GDS section too. The website has a new example, 'Nonlinear-EME: Supercontinuum', and the EME guide a section on pulsed propagation. Each section's chi(2), Kerr (chi(3)), Raman and two-photon absorption come from its materials: ``PulsedProcess(chi3=True)`` takes chi(3) from every material's ``n2`` and raises naming a material that has none, the Raman response is that of the material holding most of the Kerr overlap, and two-photon absorption (on by default where a material has a coefficient, ``tpa=False`` to turn it off) acts only on photon pairs above the bandgap. The run warns when the pulse will drift far enough to wrap around the time window, when energy reached the window's edges, and when the band leaves a material's index model, and it refuses a band where an index model has no finite value (silicon's fit has a pole at 1135 nm).
  * Nonlinear material data for the pulsed solver: ``n2`` for SiO2, SiN, LN and LN_MgO, the Raman response of fused silica (Blow and Wood) and lithium niobate (its four A1(TO) lines, Bache and Schiek), two-photon absorption coefficients for Si, AlGaAs, LN and LN_MgO, and bandgaps for those and for SiO2, SiN and GaAs, each with its reference in ``property_refs``. A user material takes the same through ``MaterialProperties(n2=, raman=, tpa=, bandgap=)``, with ``raman=(fraction, [(weight, position, half_width), ...])`` in cm^-1.
  * ``settings(bend_method='TO')`` solves a bend by transformation optics: the straight cross-section is solved with its material transformed by the bend's conformal map, instead of adding the bend factor 1 + x / R to the solver (``bend_method='cylindrical'``, the default). It works with the FDM and the FEM, in 2D and 3D sessions alike (the transformed tensor of an isotropic material is diagonal), and agrees with the cylindrical method to about 6e-4 in the effective index and 5 % in bend loss at R = 10 um. With a PML, set ``max_effective_index`` just above the guided mode: a TO bend's spectrum starts with PML modes. ``report()`` names the method next to the solver.

* Fixed

  * With ``EMODE_NONINTERACTIVE=1``, a ``plot()`` could hang. It no longer shows a window in that mode.
  * On Apple silicon, a circuit with large free-space ports could run out of memory. The sparse solver now solves the port modes in small blocks.
  * Under a PML, a lossy absorber mode could come out as mode 0, ahead of the guided mode. Guided modes now come first, and ``remove_pml_modes_bool`` is more reliable.
  * In a lossy medium, some lossy modes grew along the guide instead of decaying, so an EME chain could amplify. Every mode of a passive structure now decays.
  * ``sweep(key='wavelength', solve_type='EME')`` never returned its data.
  * A function whose first argument is not ``key``, such as ``monitor_field('plane')``, failed when that argument was given positionally.
  * ``monitor_field()`` and ``monitor_coupling()`` failed on a chain built with ``copy_section``, such as a grating.
  * A wavelength scan with the FEM solved each wavelength on a different mesh, which added about 1e-5 of noise to the effective index. Wavelength scans now share one mesh (the new setting ``fem_mesh_wavelength`` chooses its wavelength).
  * A mirrored section (``copy_section(mirror=True)``) and a bend toward -x were laid out in the wrong place.
  * The 'z-x' outline left out shapes given by a ``width`` rather than a ``mask``.
  * A symmetry wall in x, including the one ``boundary_condition='TE'`` or ``'TM'`` adds, folded a bend about x, which a bend does not allow. A bend now keeps only the vertical fold, and an explicit x wall is replaced by ``'0'`` with a warning.
  * ``plot()`` of a circuit with a loop (a ring closed with ``connect_ports``) failed. It now draws the ring's layout, cross-sections and field. ``EME()`` of a ring with no open port no longer fails.
  * Windowed ports (``Port(window=...)``): several windows on one side of a section now conserve power and are reciprocal, connected or not, each window keeps only the modes its content guides, and each is placed on its own window in the layout. Results with windowed ports move slightly.
  * A section after a bend could meet it off-center when anything upstream was offset. Sections now meet centered along any path.
  * A refined taper or GDS section with interface roughness and ``EME_settings(apply_scattering=True)`` raised an error.

* Faster

  * Refinement bisects a slice at an already-solved cross-section near its midpoint when there is one (``EME_settings(snap_window=0.25)``, a fraction of the slice, 0 to disable), so a taper or GDS section that passes through widths another one solved reuses those solves.
  * Refinement reuses a solved cross-section wherever the geometry repeats it: the mirror-image half of a taper declared as two sections, or a GDS cell that tapers up and back down. Interpolated and cut geometry is rounded to about 1e-6 nm, so the two halves no longer differ in the last bits and miss the cache (one slice in six re-solved on a mirrored taper).

**1.0.5: October 2, 2026**

* New

  * On Apple silicon, EMode's sparse direct solver is now rslab, a supernodal LU and complex-symmetric LDL\ :sup:`T` solver compiled into EMode. Mode solves return the same effective indices as with Intel MKL PARDISO. A mode solve with a PML is about 2.5 to 4.5 times faster (measured on an Apple silicon Mac against the previous release under Rosetta), and a solve without one takes the same time.
  * EMode runs natively on Apple silicon Macs, without Rosetta. There are now two Mac installers, ``EMode-<version>-Mac.pkg`` for Apple silicon and ``EMode-<version>-Mac-x86.pkg`` for Intel Macs. The Intel installer keeps MKL PARDISO. This is the last release with an Intel installer, and earlier versions stay available for download.
  * New :data:`settings` options, per profile: ``solver_threads`` caps the number of threads the sparse solver uses (0, the default, lets it choose, up to every core), and ``sparse_solver`` picks the solver (``'auto'``, ``'rslab'`` or ``'pardiso'``). The Linux, Windows and Intel Mac installers keep MKL PARDISO as their default, since it is still faster than rslab on x86 processors, and include rslab as ``sparse_solver='rslab'``. The ``EMODE_SOLVER_THREADS`` environment variable limits the thread count for every profile that does not set its own, and the 16-thread cap on the MKL thread count is gone.
  * The heat and electrostatic solves use the same solver.
  * Every installer except the Intel Mac one now meshes FEM cross-sections with the same version of the ``triangle`` mesher, so heat and electrostatic results no longer differ slightly between platforms. On Linux and Windows the mesh changes a little: the :doc:`Phase Shifter </examples/Phase_Shifter>` example's V\ :sub:`pi`\ L moves from 5.39 to 5.10 V·cm.
  * Full-vector finite-element mode solver, available with EMode\ :sup:`3D`. :data:`FEM` solves a cross-section on a triangle mesh that follows every material interface, with second-order elements, and refines the mesh where the modes need it. Boundaries, PMLs, expansion regions, bends, lossy and anisotropic materials (including crystals tilted out of the cross-section with ``theta``), and heater and electro-optic perturbations work as they do in the FDM, and FEM and FDM sections can meet at a junction. ``plot(mesh_display=True)`` draws the triangle mesh. See the new :doc:`FEM: SOI </examples/FEM_SOI>` example.
  * New :data:`solve` runs either mode solver. ``solver_method='FDM'`` or ``'FEM'`` is the same as calling :data:`FDM` or :data:`FEM`, and ``'default'`` follows the new ``solver`` setting.
  * New :data:`settings` option ``solver`` picks the mode solver for :data:`solve`, :data:`EME` and :data:`sweep`. Its default, ``'default'``, is the FDM in every session, so existing scripts solve as before. The FEM runs only when asked for, with ``solver='FEM'``, :data:`FEM` or ``solve(solver_method='FEM')``. Calling :data:`FDM` or :data:`FEM` sets ``solver`` to that method. The ``fem_*`` settings control the FEM mesh and its refinement.
  * :data:`report` names the mode solver on its wavelength line, for example ``Wavelength: 1550.0 nm    Solver: FEM``, and a saved CSV records it too.
  * Biaxial crystals. :data:`KTP` and :data:`LBO` are in the material database, each with three principal indices, its :math:`\chi^{(2)}` tensor and, for KTP, its electro-optic tensor. A third orientation angle, ``psi`` (in :class:`MaterialSpec` and :data:`add_material`), turns a crystal about its own z axis before ``theta`` and ``phi``, so a biaxial crystal can take any orientation. Omitted, ``psi`` is 0 and existing simulations are unchanged. See :class:`MaterialSpec` for the convention.
  * New :data:`settings` option ``include_scattering``: with ``include_scattering=True``, every solve of the profile (by :data:`FDM`, :data:`FEM`, :data:`solve`, :data:`EME` or :data:`sweep`) also computes the interfacial scattering loss of each rough shape. ``FDM(scattering=True)`` still does this for a single solve.
  * ``nonlinear=False`` on :data:`straight_section` or :data:`taper_section` removes a declared :math:`\chi^{(2)}` process, so the section propagates linearly again.
* Fixed

  * **Material loss now attenuates.** A lossy material (``loss=`` in dB/m, or a database material with an extinction coefficient) amplified the mode in :data:`EME`: a 200 µm lossy strip transmitted 110% of its power. Lossy modes now have an effective index with a negative imaginary part, and :data:`EME` attenuates them by exactly the reported loss. :data:`refractive_index`, :data:`permittivity` and plots still show n + jk. Profiles solved by an earlier release keep their old sign until they are solved again.
  * **Symmetric boundaries unfold correctly.** A mode solved with an 'S' or 'A' boundary was unfolded onto the full window with the same sign for every field component, but some components are odd under the mirror. Junctions between a folded and an unfolded section, the :math:`\chi^{(2)}` coupling of folded solves, plane-wave junctions and plots of unfolded fields were all affected. Each component now takes its physical mirror parity.
  * **A windowed port on a symmetry-folded profile couples correctly.** A port with ``window=`` on a profile solved with an 'S' or 'A' boundary left the mirrored half of its window at zero, so its junction was wrong wherever the window crossed the symmetry plane. Two ports folded the same way at different transverse offsets had the same problem.
  * The :math:`\chi^{(2)}` coupling of symmetry-folded solves (SHG, SFG, DFG, SPDC) used the pump's symmetry for a signal folded differently from the pump. It now matches a full-window solve.
  * :data:`chi2_spectrum` left the profiles it re-solved with no scattering data, so ``get('scattering_loss')`` and :data:`report` read zero after a scan. The re-solved profiles now keep their per-mode scattering loss.
  * **An FDM solve asked for more modes than a structure guides returns its lossy modes.** Past the guided and radiation modes, the solve returned modes with an effective index near 0, and at higher mode counts it failed with "array must not contain infs or NaNs". The next modes are now the lossy ones, with an imaginary effective index, ordered from least to most decay. Guided modes are unchanged.
  * The FDM solve returns the same modes on every run. Degenerate modes used to come back as a different mixture each time.
  * **FDM '0' walls are metal on all four sides.** The bottom wall was a magnetic wall half a cell outside the window, and ``field_to_solve='Ht'`` returned modes the ``'Et'`` solve did not. Both now return a metal waveguide's modes. Modes kept away from the walls by a cladding or a PML are unchanged. Modes that reach a wall move: the :doc:`EME: Taper </examples/EME_Taper>` example's printed indices change by up to 3e-4.
  * A taper whose etch mask is offset from the waveguide center re-centered the mask on every interpolated slice, so the geometry jumped to the center mid-taper. A misspelled :data:`shape` keyword now raises an error instead of being ignored.
  * A :data:`shape` stacked on a layer that came out empty, such as a fully etched layer, was placed half that layer's height too high. It now sits directly on top of it.
  * ``FDM(section=..., propagation_distance=...)`` failed on a straight or bend section, or any section whose cross-section does not change along its length.
  * :data:`group_index` crashed on an FDM profile.
  * A junction between two differently gridded sections (a confined port, a PML against a plain window, a different resolution or offset) resampled both modes onto a common grid smaller than either and shifted by half a cell. A confined port's S-matrix was off by up to 6e-2. The common grid now covers both sections exactly.
  * :data:`get` reads back every setting :data:`settings` accepts, including older aliases such as ``radius``. Before, only ``wavelength``, ``background_material`` and ``BC`` worked. :data:`inspect` lists every name :data:`get` can read and takes ``profile=``.
  * :data:`overlap` stores its result on the profile named by ``profile_b`` instead of always on the default profile, and no longer fails when the default profile has several wavelengths. The new ``get('overlaps')`` keeps one record per pair of modes, so overlaps no longer overwrite each other.
  * :data:`label_profile` works on a multi-wavelength profile.
  * ``FDM(verbose=False)`` still printed its progress. It is now quiet.
  * The ``mode_filter`` and ``fLanczos`` arguments of :data:`FDM` had no effect. They now reach the solver, and a bad value raises an error.
  * The ``eme_scattering`` option of :data:`settings` never had an effect. It is deprecated: it is still accepted, with a warning that it does nothing. Use ``EME_settings(apply_scattering=True)`` to apply scattering loss in :data:`EME`.
  * The ``permeability`` and ``fill_permeability`` arguments of :data:`shape` raised an unclear error. The error now explains that a shape's permeability comes from its material, as in ``material=MaterialProperties(n=1.5, mu=2.0)``.
  * A simulation name that is not a valid file name on every platform, such as ``'run:1'``, is rejected by :data:`init` and :data:`open` with an error that names the problem, instead of producing a simulation file that cannot be written on Windows.
  * :data:`FDM` failed on a multi-wavelength profile, from a wavelength list in :data:`settings` or from :data:`create_profile_set`. Each wavelength is now solved at its own wavelength and :data:`get` returns the results keyed by wavelength. :data:`EME` on a wavelength list in :data:`settings` failed too.
  * On a multi-wavelength profile, the results of :data:`get`, :data:`effective_area`, :data:`group_index`, :data:`orthogonality`, :data:`scattering`, :data:`confinement` and :data:`report` could not be indexed by wavelength: ``result[1550.0]`` raised a KeyError. They are now a read-only dict keyed by wavelength, in which ``result[1550]`` and ``result[1550.0]`` are the same entry. :data:`confinement` returns its dictionary of confinement factors per shape directly, as documented, instead of inside a one-element list, so a script that used ``confinement()[0]`` should drop the ``[0]``.
  * A material given by its permittivity, ``MaterialSpec(material=MaterialProperties(eps=...))``, ignored its ``theta`` and ``phi``, while its ``d`` tensor was rotated by them. Its permittivity now turns with it.
  * :data:`shape` with a single value for ``roughness_rms`` or ``correlation_length`` raised an error, although the documentation says a single value applies to all interfaces. It now does, and a list of any length other than two raises an error that names the argument.
  * A lossy anisotropic material rotated off its principal axes (``loss`` with ``theta``, ``phi`` or ``psi``) had its loss applied to every element of its index tensor, which made the loss depend on polarization. The loss is now applied to the diagonal only, as for the unrotated material.
  * :data:`heat` followed by a mode solve failed with an IndexError when a rotated anisotropic material had a nonzero ``dn_dT``. The heated index of such a material is now its index tensor shifted by ``dn_dT`` times the temperature change.
  * :data:`refractive_index` with an array of wavelengths failed for a rotated anisotropic material, and for exactly three wavelengths returned a wrong result. It now returns one 3x3 tensor per wavelength.

**1.0.4: September 24, 2026**

* New

  * Second-order nonlinear EME, available with EMode3D. A :data:`straight_section` or :data:`taper_section` takes ``nonlinear=`` with an ``SHGProcess``, ``SFGProcess``, or ``DFGProcess`` naming the process wavelengths. Quasi-phase matching is declared with ``poling_period`` (plus ``duty_cycle`` and ``qpm_order``) on the process, or built by hand from alternating ``inverted=True`` sections, the two routes agree.
  * A CW pump is declared with ``settings(excitation=Source(port, wavelength, mode, power, phase))``. :data:`EME` still returns the small-signal S-matrix, and now also solves the power-dependent response, available as a ``CircuitResponse`` from ``get('response')``, outgoing and incoming amplitudes at every port, radiated power per wavelength, and convergence. ``get('sources')`` returns the declared excitation.
  * :data:`chi2_spectrum` returns a nonlinear conversion spectrum (signal out over pump in, at every wavelength asked for) from a handful of mode solves. A :math:`\chi^{(2)}` spectrum oscillates too fast with the phase mismatch to interpolate, but what the coupled-mode equations consume (the coupling and the propagation constants) varies smoothly, so the modes are solved only where the mode list is still moving and the response is then evaluated point by point from interpolated coupling. Against a directly solved 13-point spectrum across a phase-matching peak, five solves reproduce every point to four decimals, sidelobes included. Quasi-phase-matched sections are included: the grating enters as a wavevector shift and a Fourier coefficient, neither of which depends on wavelength, so a poled section keeps its exact grating while its phase mismatch moves with the interpolated propagation constants.
  * ``sweep(key='wavelength', values=..., adaptive=True)`` does the same for an ordinary sweep. ``values`` keeps its meaning (the wavelengths you want results at) and ``adaptive`` changes only where the solver runs: modes are matched between neighbouring solves through a phase-corrected overlap, and a bisection in wavelength adds solves until the mode list stops moving by more than ``tolerance``. Any stretch it could not resolve is reported in ``unconverged`` instead of raising.
  * Free-space excitation: :data:`free_space_field` declares a Gaussian beam launched into a port. The beam is projected onto that port's modes and drives the field plot through ``plot(excitation=name)`` or the plot window's Mode menu. :data:`free_space_field_diagnostics` returns the fraction of the beam's power the port's modes capture, and the solver warns when it is below 90%. ``tilt`` is the angle inside the medium the beam travels through.
  * Angled facet ports: :func:`make_angled_facet_port` tilts a port's cross-section about a hinge line, so a facet's reflection back into the guided mode falls off with tilt as it does physically (see :ref:`ports`). A facet cannot sit on a :data:`pwd_section` or on a profile with a PML. ``sweep(key='section, NAME, facet_angle')`` sweeps that tilt.
  * The solver warns at a facet junction when the waveguide's ``num_modes`` is too small to hold the deflected reflection and R + T under-runs.
  * Junctions whose two sides carry different numbers of modes (every junction between a waveguide section and a :data:`pwd_section`) now solve the mode-matching equations for the reflected and transmitted amplitudes jointly instead of eliminating the reflected ones first. The two closures coincide when the counts match, so taper and GDS staircases are unchanged. At a waveguide-to-free-space junction the reflection into the fundamental moves by 10-15% (down), the answer no longer depends on the order the two sections were declared, and declaring an angled facet at zero tilt no longer changes it.
  * Per-junction settings: a :data:`connect_ports` entry accepts a third element, ``JunctionOverride(junction_normalization=...)``, applying to that junction alone.
  * Field plots: the color scale is normalized to the excitation plane's field family, with a 'Norm' selector (vector or component), the log scale spans -60 dB to 0, an 'abs sum' cross-axis reduction.
  * ``plot(profile=...)`` renders a stored profile on a simulation that has EME sections declared. Previously the plotter always followed the sections.
  * :data:`get_fields` with no ``key`` returns all six E and H components.
  * :data:`material_explorer` with ``file_name=`` saves the figure without opening a window, like :data:`plot`.
  * Setting ``EMODE_NONINTERACTIVE=1`` makes every :data:`plot` build its figure and return instead of blocking on the window. ``EMODE_EMAIL`` and ``EMODE_PASSWORD`` log in without the login window, for unattended runs.
  * EMode prints a notice when it checks out a license and a newer release is available. It turns itself on once a release's installers are downloadable.
  * The default MKL thread count is capped at 16 (an explicit ``MKL_NUM_THREADS`` overrides this setting).
  * Second-order nonlinear coefficients (``d``) added for GaP, AlN, and InGaP, and for the new materials GaN, LiTaO3, and ZnSe, each with a citation, the BBO, GaAs, AlGaAs, LN, and LN_MgO entries are corrected and cited. :data:`d_matrix` with ``reference=True`` returns the citation. ``get('<material>')`` now includes ``property_refs``, the source of every non-optical and nonlinear value.
  * Electro-optic (Pockels) coefficients ``r`` for LN, LN_MgO, GaAs, InP, AlN, and GaN. :data:`electrostatic` derives the DC field from the solved potential, and :data:`FDM` then solves the biased waveguide through the r tensor (``include_static``), so a phase shifter's V\ :sub:`pi`\ L comes straight out of :data:`report`.
  * Thermal and electrical properties (``thermal_conductivity``, ``dn_dT``, ``eps_r_dc``, and ``resistivity`` for metals) filled in for every database material, plus the heater and electrode metals Au, Al, Cu, Ti, W, Cr, TiN, and NiCr with their optical constants at 1550 nm. The :doc:`Material Database </emodeguide/materials>` page tabulates every material's non-optical properties, :math:`\chi^{(2)}` and electro-optic coefficients, and their sources.
  * :data:`report` and :data:`plot` mark modes that live in the PML: the report gains a ``PML`` column and the plot's mode menu reads ``3, TE, PML``. The designation is available as ``PML_indices`` and ``window_confinement``.
  * New setting ``pml_seed_bool``: a PML simulation is first solved without its PML, and the eigensolver is then seeded with that fundamental mode, so the guided mode lands at index 0 without a hand-tuned ``max_effective_index``.
  * New website examples: :doc:`Nonlinear-EME: SHG </examples/EME_SHG>` (GaAs-on-insulator second-harmonic generation, straight and tapered, with both devices' conversion-efficiency spectra), :doc:`Nonlinear-EME: PPLN </examples/EME_PPLN>` (a periodically poled thin-film lithium niobate waveguide doubling a telecom TE pump, with propagation loss from sidewall and surface roughness), :doc:`EME: Angled Facet </examples/EME_Angled_Facet>` (a lensed-fiber Gaussian into an angled-facet taper, swept over facet tilt), :doc:`Heater </examples/Heater>` (a Pt heater above an SOI waveguide, with the power for a :math:`\pi` phase shift), and :doc:`Phase Shifter </examples/Phase_Shifter>` (Au electrodes beside an x-cut LNOI waveguide, with V\ :sub:`pi`\ L).
  * The :doc:`Variable Descriptions </emodeguide/variables>` page now explains how the simulation file is organized (top-level keys vs. the ``profiles`` dictionary, default and named profiles, multi-wavelength profile sets) and groups variables by where they live. :data:`inspect` is documented.

  * New website examples: :doc:`Adaptive Scan </examples/Adaptive_Scan>` (``sweep(adaptive=True)``, a fine wavelength spectrum from a sparse set of solves) and :doc:`Nonlinear-EME: DFG </examples/EME_DFG>`, difference-frequency generation in periodically poled lithium niobate, with two simultaneous ``Source`` excitations and a three-wavelength profile set.
  * A sweep or a :data:`chi2_spectrum` no longer prints the mesh and eigensolve of every wavelength it solves. One line for the scan replaces one pair per solve.
  * A :math:`\chi^{(2)}` section warns when its mode basis still contains PML modes: their large imaginary propagation constants make the coupled-mode solve stiff, and the warning names ``remove_pml_modes_bool``, which drops them.
  * ``get('response').power_vs_z(wavelength)`` returns the forward power against position along a :math:`\chi^{(2)}` section, from one solve. The coupled-amplitude ODE already integrates along z and used to discard the result, so a pump or second-harmonic power curve cost one full :data:`EME` per length. The forward amplitudes are now recorded after the driven solve converges and returned with the response, stitched across the slices of a refined taper so the curve reads as one. ``amplitude_vs_z`` gives the per-mode complex amplitudes behind it. Sampling is controlled by ``z_samples`` on the process (default 100 points per slice, 0 to switch it off).
  * :data:`plot` with ``plane='z-x'`` on a :math:`\chi^{(2)}` section draws the driven field. It uses the declared ``excitation`` rather than a unit mode, since conversion depends on pump power, and renders each slice from the amplitudes recorded along it, so the generated wavelength builds along the guide instead of stepping at slice boundaries. ``wavelength=`` picks which wavelength to show. Without a declared excitation the field shown is the small-signal response and the solver says so.
  * The coupled-amplitude ODE's default tolerances are ``solver_rtol=1e-6`` and ``solver_atol=1e-9``, with ``RK45`` as the default method. Pass ``solver_rtol``, ``solver_atol`` or ``solver_method`` on the process to override any of them. The explicit Runge-Kutta methods (``RK45``, ``RK23``, ``DOP853``) are the usable choices. The coupled amplitudes are complex, and SciPy's implicit solvers (``LSODA``, ``Radau``, ``BDF``) reject a complex initial condition.
* Fixed

  * :data:`scattering` corrupted the profile it had just reported on, so every later :data:`get` on that simulation failed. The same path also wrote into a material object returned by ``get('<material>')``.
  * ``get('scattering_loss')`` (and ``sweep(result='scattering_loss')``) returned the per-shape metadata dictionary instead of the loss, and raised an error when more than one shape carried roughness.
  * In an EMode2D session every internal error was reported as "only available with EMode3D". An unexpected internal error now names only the function that was called.
  * A saved simulation could not always be reopened: port references came back as plain tuples, section declarations reloaded as dictionaries, and some stored types were unknown to the reader unless that session happened to have imported them first.
  * ``sweep(result=['mode_order'])`` raised an error, and an unsupported section key failed an assertion instead of naming the key.
  * A saved simulation reopened only if the reading session happened to have already imported the module defining each stored type. Otherwise it failed with a bare ``KeyError`` naming an internal class. Every tagged type is now imported before the file is read, and an unknown tag raises a proper error.
  * A :data:`pwd_section` with an offset window is drawn where its field is.
  * The z panel of a field plot is normalized to the excitation plane, not the global maximum.
  * A forbidden section name raised an opaque "unexpected exception" instead of the intended error, and a bad value in :data:`EME_settings` or a section's ``settings=`` did the same. :data:`create_profile_set` raised an error on first use.
  * :data:`reset` accepted any ``kind`` and silently did nothing for a misspelled one. It now raises an error. ``reset('shapes')`` also clears the stale mesh and mode data, so the next :data:`FDM` solves the new geometry instead of the old one.
  * :data:`import_database` failed on a citation containing ``=``, rejected integer ``wavelength_range`` entries, and defaulted the wavelength range to 200-2000 um instead of 0.2-2.0 um.
  * ``get(key, profile=...)`` returned the default profile's value for every per-profile variable regardless of ``profile``. A multi-wavelength profile now returns a dict keyed by wavelength.
  * :data:`refractive_index` on an unknown material raised a raw error. :data:`permeability` returned a nested list for database materials and crashed on numeric input. :data:`create_profile_set` split a bare string into characters and rejected its own documented default set name. :data:`get_shape` crashed before any :data:`scattering`/:data:`confinement` call.
  * :data:`report` returned a dictionary that paired column headers with mode rows and ignored its ``profile`` argument. It now returns one list per column for the requested profile.
  * The ``emode`` command line no longer prints a redundant "Opening the EMode Login window..." message.
  * An exception inside :data:`sweep` left its output-suppression flag set, silencing every later operation in that session.
  * :data:`scattering` (and any ``FDM(scattering=True)``) failed with "'FDM' failed with an unexpected internal error" on a profile solved with ``remove_pml_modes_bool``. Dropping the absorber's modes leaves the basis shorter than ``num_modes`` asked for, but the per-mode loop still ran over the requested count and indexed past the end of the field arrays. A mode explicitly requested beyond the solved basis now raises an error naming ``mode_list``.

**1.0.3: August 18, 2026**

* New

  * Added the setting ``pml_thickness``, which specifies the PML by physical thickness in nm instead of by a layer count. It is [north, south, east, west] like every other four-sided setting, and 0 on a side falls back to ``num_pml_layers`` there, so the two can be mixed per side. A layer count measures grid cells, so the absorber shrinks as the mesh is refined. ``pml_thickness`` holds it fixed, which is what a resolution convergence sweep needs. See :data:`settings`.

* Fixed

  * A simulation with a PML could not be reopened: :data:`open` failed before any plot or solve ran, and the ``.eph`` file was already wrong when it was written.
  * A boundary condition that pins the field at the wall left phantom modes in the mode list: one grid column wide, with exactly zero loss, sorted in among the physical modes. They are removed from the eigenvalue problem now, and genuine modes appear in the slots they were occupying.
  * The PML now delivers the thickness its profile specifies. The grid gave the absorber smoothed cell widths, so a default 10-layer PML measured 492.8 nm against a design value of 500.0 nm, and the outermost (most absorbing) layer absorbed 14% less than intended. Reported radiation loss shifts by a few percent in simulations with a PML.
  * The cached-solution key discarded the PML coordinate stretch, so two profiles differing only in their PML could share a junction S-matrix.
  * Lowering ``num_modes`` with :data:`settings` after a solve produced a mode list that EME could not assemble.
  * ``expansion_size`` validation checked every direction against the north expansion instead of its own, so a valid single-direction expansion could be rejected and a bad resolution could escape the check.
  * :data:`FDM` called without a ``profile`` argument no longer also writes a junk profile named 'None' alongside the default one.
  * A junction between a section with a symmetric or antisymmetric boundary condition and either a :data:`pwd_section` or a differently reduced neighbor now integrates every overlap over the same domain.
  * A windowed port lost its normalization when its basis passed through a transform, giving a 13% error in the self-overlap on a symmetry-folded port.
  * A port with a composed ``basis_map`` raised an error instead of producing a cache key.
  * Overlap products are recomputed after the field data changes instead of being served from a stale cache.

**1.0.2: August 5, 2026**

* New

  * Free space sections using plane-wave decomposition, declared with :data:`pwd_section`. Junctions with neighboring waveguide sections are computed analytically instead of by a mode solve.
  * Generalized port definitions and connections, including branching: a ``ports`` argument on every section function, :data:`connect_ports` for wiring named ports explicitly, and the :class:`Port` and :class:`Bounds` classes in emodeconnection (see :ref:`ports`). A connection also positions its two sections relative to each other, replacing the now legacy per-section ``offset``.

  * The innermost PML layer was transparent, so a PML of N layers had only N-1 absorbing layers, and the PML parameters were missing from the cached-solution key, so two profiles differing only in their PML could share a solution.
  * Shape geometry is now resolved in absolute coordinates, independent of the simulation window.

  * In EME plots, section outlines disagreed with the field for every section after the first in a chain with transverse offsets.
